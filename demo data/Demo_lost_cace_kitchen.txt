WHY THIS CALL WAS LOST — COACHING NOTES
================================================================================

This is the most instructive loss in the dataset. The restaurant was a genuine
fit. The rep had a real local reference the prospect actually recognized. They
had the prospect on the phone for 5 minutes. They still lost it, entirely on
execution.

THE FIT CASE (what a prepared rep would have known before dialing):
- The Cace Kitchen has no online ordering. They take orders by phone only.
- They have no delivery presence on DoorDash, Uber Eats, or any third party.
- They have a website (thecacekitchen.com) but no ordering infrastructure.
- A Mexican full-service restaurant in Longview with no digital ordering
  footprint is exactly the profile Owner converts. The pain is there. The
  revenue opportunity is there. The fit is real.

THE EXECUTION FAILURES:

1. NO PRE-CALL RESEARCH USED ON THE CALL: The rep knew the restaurant was
   near Breakfast Club, which is a strong opener. But they had clearly not
   looked at the Cace Kitchen's website, Google rank, or online presence
   before calling. When the prospect said business was fine, the rep had no
   specific data to push back with. A prepared rep would have said: "I pulled
   your report before calling, and when someone searches best Mexican food in
   Longview you're not showing up in the top results, even though you've been
   in business 40 years. That's the gap we fix."

2. DISCOVERY ABANDONED TOO EARLY: The rep asked one qualifying question ("you
   don't have any delivery, right?") and when the prospect said no online
   ordering, the rep pivoted straight to agreeing and never explored the
   underlying business situation. They never asked: how are new customers
   finding you? What does a slow Tuesday look like? Are you seeing customers
   come from outside Longview? These questions would have surfaced the pain
   that the prospect's final responses actually hint at.

3. ACCEPTED THE "40 YEARS" OBJECTION WITHOUT A RESPONSE: The prospect said
   three times that the business had been running the same way for 40 years
   and they wanted to keep it that way. This is a standard comfort-zone
   objection, not a real disqualifier. A top rep uses it as an opening: "That
   track record is exactly why this makes sense. The customers you've built
   loyalty with over 40 years, you currently have no way to reach them
   directly when they're not in the building. We fix that." The rep instead
   validated the objection and accepted it.

4. NO URGENCY AND NO SPECIFIC CONSEQUENCE: The rep never told the prospect
   what they were losing by staying phone-only. No mention of competitors
   showing up above them on Google. No estimate of orders going to other
   Mexican spots in Longview. No number attached to the opportunity. The
   prospect heard a pitch with no stakes.

5. CLOSED ON THE WRONG THING: The rep's last real attempt was "I could at
   least show you how you could capture [money on the table]." That's a weak,
   hedged ask. A confident close would have been: "Griffin at Breakfast Club
   was in the exact same spot, phone orders only, before we started working
   together. Can I get you 20 minutes next week to show you what changed for
   him?" Instead the rep accepted the final no and ended the call.

KEY LESSON: Comfort with the status quo is the most common objection in the
dataset, and it is almost never a real disqualifier. The rep who arrives with
specific data (your Google rank, your competitors, what Griffin's numbers
looked like before vs. after) can make the status quo feel costly. The rep
who arrives empty-handed can only accept it.
